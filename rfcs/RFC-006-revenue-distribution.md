# RFC-006: Revenue Distribution

**Status:** DRAFT
**Category:** Protocol / Financial
**Target:** KeystoneGrid v1

## 1. Summary

This RFC defines how revenue generated by a Keystone Asset may be distributed to eligible ownership-interest holders.

Distribution accounting must be deterministic and independently verifiable.

## 2. Core Principle

Where distributions are protocol-enforced, entitlement must derive from authoritative ownership state and a clearly defined ownership basis.

The backend may calculate, index, report, and notify.

It must not arbitrarily rewrite authoritative entitlement.

## 3. Distribution Lifecycle

A distribution may follow:

`ANNOUNCED → FUNDED → AVAILABLE → CLAIMED → COMPLETED`

An explicit failure or cancellation state may be introduced where required.

## 4. Distribution Record

A distribution should identify:

* asset;
* distribution ID;
* funding/payment asset;
* total distributable amount;
* ownership basis;
* eligibility rule;
* creation time;
* funding status;
* claim state;
* relevant ledger references.

## 5. Ownership Basis

Every distribution must explicitly define how ownership determines entitlement.

Possible models include:

### Current Ownership

Entitlement is calculated using ownership at the time of claim.

### Snapshot Ownership

Entitlement is calculated using ownership at a defined ledger/time snapshot.

### Period-Based Ownership

Entitlement is calculated using ownership over a defined period.

Keystone must not mix these models implicitly.

## 6. Snapshot Requirement

For distributions where ownership can change between announcement and claim, a deterministic snapshot mechanism is preferred.

The snapshot must be:

* identifiable;
* reproducible;
* auditable;
* tied to protocol state.

## 7. Funding

A distribution must not become claimable merely because the backend marks it as funded.

Funding must be verifiable.

The protocol should establish that sufficient payment funds are available before claims are accepted.

## 8. Claim Model

Where a pull-based claim mechanism is used:

1. holder proves or is recognized as eligible;
2. entitlement is calculated deterministically;
3. claim is authorized;
4. payment is transferred;
5. claimed state is recorded.

A holder must not be able to claim the same entitlement twice.

## 9. Precision and Rounding

Distribution calculations must define:

* ownership unit precision;
* payment precision;
* rounding behavior;
* minimum claim;
* dust handling.

Rounding must be deterministic.

The same inputs must always produce the same result.

## 10. Dust

Small undistributable remainders must have an explicit policy.

Possible policies include:

* carry-forward;
* issuer retention;
* treasury allocation;
* subsequent distribution;
* defined rounding tolerance.

The policy must be disclosed and deterministic.

## 11. Backend Responsibilities

The backend may provide:

* distribution discovery;
* expected entitlement estimates;
* historical distributions;
* notification;
* indexing;
* reconciliation;
* reporting.

Backend calculations should be independently reproducible from protocol state.

## 12. Frontend Requirements

The frontend should distinguish:

* announced;
* funded;
* available;
* claimed;
* completed.

Users should be able to understand whether an amount is:

* projected;
* calculated;
* claimable;
* already claimed.

## 13. Events

The protocol should expose economically meaningful events such as:

* distribution created;
* distribution funded;
* distribution made available;
* distribution claimed;
* distribution completed.

## 14. Core Invariants

* Total claims cannot exceed distributable funds.
* A claim cannot be executed twice.
* Ownership basis is deterministic.
* Unauthorized accounts cannot claim.
* Backend data cannot increase authoritative entitlement.
* A distribution cannot be claimable without satisfying its funding conditions.

## 15. Security Considerations

Implementation must consider:

* reentrancy;
* duplicate claims;
* rounding exploitation;
* snapshot manipulation;
* unauthorized funding;
* insufficient funding;
* compromised administrative accounts;
* stale ownership indexes;
* malicious claim parameters.

## 16. Deferred Decisions

Implementation specifications must determine:

* snapshot architecture;
* payment assets;
* claim mechanism;
* automatic versus pull-based distributions;
* distribution frequency;
* fee model;
* dust policy;
* emergency withdrawal/recovery;
* tax/reporting integrations.

## 17. Acceptance Criteria

RFC-006 is satisfied when distribution entitlement, ownership basis, funding, claims, precision, and reconciliation are deterministic and auditable.

## 18. Final Principle

**Revenue follows the protocol-defined ownership basis—not whatever balance happens to exist in an application database.**
